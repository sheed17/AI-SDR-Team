# Neyma Freight ICP

## Purpose

Define who Neyma Freight should research, qualify, and draft outreach for in v0.

Neyma Freight is an internal, operator-led AI SDR workflow for finding small freight brokerages with visible carrier-invoice, POD, billing, or reconciliation pain. It does not send outreach automatically.

## Locked Target

Target companies:

- Truckload freight brokerages
- Approx. 5-20 staff, with 10-40 acceptable when the operation is still lean
- Roughly 100-500 completed loads/month when volume can be inferred
- Operators using a TMS such as McLeod, Aljex, TAI, TMW, ARK, EZ Loader, or a spreadsheet-backed process
- Teams where carrier invoices, PODs, lumper receipts, billing, settlements, or carrier payables appear to move through email, fax, portals, or manual review

Decision-maker titles:

- Founder
- Owner
- President
- COO
- VP Operations
- Operations Manager
- Accounting Manager
- AP Manager
- Controller

Secondary contacts only when a primary decision-maker is unavailable:

- Brokerage Operations Specialist
- Billing Specialist
- Carrier Payables Specialist
- Settlements Specialist
- Load Entry Specialist
- Operations Coordinator

## Locked Pain

The prospect likely spends staff time reconciling carrier invoices against rate confirmations and completed-load documents by hand.

Common documents:

- Rate confirmation already stored in the TMS
- Carrier invoice or freight bill
- Bill of lading
- Proof of delivery
- Lumper receipt
- Accessorial backup
- Detention, layover, TONU, or re-delivery paperwork

The core gap is not generic document entry. The rate con usually already exists in the TMS, while the carrier invoice, POD, lumper receipt, and accessorial backup arrive through email, fax, carrier portal, or a shared inbox. The back-office operator toggles between the TMS, inbox, carrier portal, and document folders to decide whether the carrier should be paid.

The ROI story is margin recovery. A meaningful share of carrier invoices include a line item that does not match the rate con, often because carrier billing systems auto-apply template accessorials. Catching those mismatches before payment is more valuable than simply saving typing time.

## Public Pain Signals

Signal A: public operating workflow that suggests carrier-payables or document intake friction.

Examples:

- Instructions to email invoices, PODs, billing paperwork, claims, or accounting questions
- Carrier packet, onboarding, payment, or billing pages that route documents through email/fax
- Website language showing a carrier network, truckload brokerage, expedited, refrigerated, flatbed, drayage, or spot-market brokerage operation
- Public mention of TMS usage, load tracking, carrier portal, billing portal, or document
